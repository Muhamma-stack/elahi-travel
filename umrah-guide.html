Umrah Guide

How to Perform Umrah — Step by Step

A simple, Sunnah-based guide for our pilgrims: the rituals in order, the gates of Masjid al-Haram, the Hateem, where to leave your luggage, help for children and elderly parents — everything that makes your Umrah easier.

Home/Umrah Guide
In Short

Umrah in Four Steps

The whole journey is four steps, in this exact order. Remember the order and everything else becomes easy.

01

1. Ihram & Niyyah

Wear Ihram at the Miqat, make the intention for Umrah in your heart and begin the Talbiyah.

02

2. Tawaf

Seven circuits around the Kaaba, keeping it on your left, starting and finishing at the Black Stone.

03

3. Sa'i

Seven trips between Safa and Marwa — finishing at Marwa. Any dhikr or Qur'an is fine.

04

4. Halq or Taqsir

Men shave or trim the hair, women cut a fingertip's length. Umrah is complete.

Quick reminder: the Talbiyah is recited from the moment you enter Ihram until you begin Tawaf — then you stop it. Tawaf → two rak'ah → Sa'i → cut the hair, and never cut the hair before Sa'i.

Preparation

Before You Start: Ihram & Niyyah

Most mistakes happen before Tawaf even begins — so take a few minutes to get this part right.

01

Getting into Ihram

  • Take a ghusl (or wudu) first, then — before wearing Ihram — you may apply perfume to your head and beard. Applying perfume after the intention is not allowed.
  • Men: two unstitched white sheets — one around the waist (izar, held with a belt) and one over the shoulders (rida'), plus sandals. No stitched clothing, no head cover, no gloves.
  • Women: ordinary modest clothing that covers the body and hair. No face-veil or gloves while in Ihram, and no special two-sheet garment.
  • Pray two rak'ah if it is not a disliked time, then make the intention in your heart — Labbayk Allahumma bi 'Umrah ("Here I am, O Allah, for Umrah"). The intention is not said out loud.
  • Miqat: if you are travelling from Riyadh by road, you enter Ihram at the Miqat on the way (Qarn al-Manazil / As-Sayl). Pilgrims flying in wear Ihram before the Miqat announced on the flight. Our team will tell you exactly where the bus stops.
Talbiyah

لَبَّيْكَ اللَّهُمَّ لَبَّيْكَ، لَبَّيْكَ لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ لَبَّيْكَ، إِنَّ الْحَمْدَ وَالنِّعْمَةَ لَكَ وَالْمُلْكَ لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ

Labbayka Allahumma labbayk, labbayka la sharika laka labbayk, inna al-hamda wan-ni'mata laka wal-mulk, la sharika lak.

"Here I am, O Allah, here I am. Here I am — You have no partner, here I am. Indeed all praise, blessing and dominion belong to You. You have no partner."

02

Not allowed while in Ihram

  • Cutting hair or nails, and shaving any part of the body.
  • Perfume, scented soap, creams or scented tissues.
  • Marital relations, or touching with desire.
  • Men: stitched clothes, covering the head, gloves. Women: face-veil and gloves.
  • Hunting, and cutting trees or plants inside the Haram.

If it happens by mistake or out of ignorance, stop as soon as you remember — there is no penalty for a mistake.

Sunnah Method

Tawaf, Then Sa'i, Then Cut the Hair

Take your time. Tawaf and Sa'i usually take one and a half to three hours at a normal pace — there is no need to rush.

1

Tawaf — seven circuits around the Kaaba

  • Start in line with the Black Stone (Hajar al-Aswad). If you can reach it, touch it with your right hand and kiss it; if it is crowded, just face it, raise your hand and say Allahu akbar.
  • Keep the Kaaba on your left and walk anticlockwise. Each circuit starts and ends in line with the Black Stone — seven complete circuits.
  • Men walk briskly with short steps (ramal) in the first three circuits and normally in the last four. Men may also leave the right shoulder uncovered (idtiba) during Tawaf.
  • At the Yemeni corner you may touch it with your hand if it is easy — do not kiss it and do not point at it.
  • Between the Yemeni corner and the Black Stone recite: Rabbana atina fid-dunya hasanah wa fil-akhirati hasanah wa qina 'adhab an-nar.
  • For the rest of the Tawaf you may make any dhikr or dua, or recite Qur'an. There is no fixed dua for each circuit.
  • Tawaf is valid on the ground floor or on the upper floors inside Masjid al-Haram. If your wudu breaks, repeat the wudu and continue — most scholars require wudu for Tawaf.
Very important: walk around the outside of the Hateem wall (Hijr Ismail). Passing between the Kaaba and that wall during Tawaf cancels that circuit.
2

After Tawaf — two rak'ah and Zamzam

  • Pray two rak'ah behind Maqam Ibrahim if there is space, otherwise anywhere in the mosque — in the first rak'ah after al-Fatihah recite al-Kafirun, and in the second al-Ikhlas.
  • Drink Zamzam and ask Allah for good in this life and the next — Zamzam dispensers are on every level, so no need to queue at one spot.
  • Then set off for Safa. Touching the Black Stone again on the way is good if it is easy — otherwise walk past without pointing.
3

Sa'i — seven trips between Safa and Marwa

  • When you are near Safa, recite Inna as-Safa wal-Marwata min sha'a'irillah once, and start with Safa (Safa to Marwa counts as one trip).
  • Face the Kaaba, praise Allah and make dua, then walk. Between the two green markers men jog, and women walk normally the whole way.
  • Complete seven trips and finish at Marwa. As in Tawaf, there is no fixed dua for each trip — any dhikr, dua or Qur'an is fine.
  • A wheelchair or an electric cart is allowed for Sa'i and Tawaf if you cannot walk — see the wheelchair section below.
4

Halq or Taqsir — ending the Ihram

  • Men shave the whole head (halq — the better option) or trim hair from all over the head, not just one side.
  • Women never shave — they cut about a fingertip's length (roughly 2 cm) from the ends of the hair, a little from each braid if the hair is braided.
  • Hair can be cut in Makkah or later. Once it is done your Umrah is complete and everything that was forbidden in Ihram becomes allowed again.

For the Kaaba & Mataf

The five principal gates open straight into the Mataf: 1, 45, 62/63, 79 and 100. They have twin minarets, which makes them easy to recognise.

For Safa & Marwa

Use Bab as-Safa (12) to start Sa'i and Bab al-Marwah (23) to leave from Marwa after you finish.

For Zamzam

There is no single Zamzam gate — chilled and room-temperature dispensers with cups are on every level and near the main gates.

The Hateem

Hijr Ismail — the Curved Wall Beside the Kaaba

The Hateem (also called Hijr Ismail or al-Hatim) is the low, semi-circular white marble wall on the north / north-west side of the Kaaba, standing a short distance from the Kaaba but not touching it. The space between that wall and the Kaaba was part of the Kaaba built by Prophet Ibrahim (peace be upon him), and the Prophet ﷺ told Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) to pray inside it "because it is part of the House".

  • You can pray inside it. No Ihram, permit or special booking is needed — you simply walk in.
  • How to enter: the curved wall is open at both ends. During Tawaf you pass alongside it — when you reach the north side, step through the opening on either side of the wall (the entrance nearest to you), pray inside, and come back out the same way.
  • Which gates bring you closest? Any gate that opens into the Mataf — King Abdulaziz (1), Bab al-Fath (45), Bab al-Umrah (62/63) and King Fahd (79). From there, walk to the Kaaba and go round to its north side, where the curved wall stands.
  • Never cut through it during Tawaf. Passing between the Kaaba and the Hateem wall breaks that circuit — the space is treated as part of the Kaaba.
  • At busy times the entry is controlled and may be closed (after Fajr, on Fridays and in Ramadan). If the entrance is shut, ask a marshal — they open it as soon as the crowd allows.
Making It Easy

Luggage, Children & Elderly Parents

Coming by bus from Riyadh usually means carrying a bag. Here is how to handle it, and how to keep the little ones and the elders comfortable.

Luggage — where to leave your bags

  • With us, first: if you are travelling on our bus, your luggage stays safely in the bus (or in your hotel room if you booked a hotel) while you perform Umrah. Always keep your money, passport and phone with you.
  • Free official storage: the Haram authority offers free luggage storage, open 24 hours, for up to about 4 hours. Bags only (nothing loose in sacks), roughly 7 kg per bag, and no valuables, food or medicine.
  • Where the storage points are: reported near the eastern courtyard by the Makkah Library, the western courtyard near Gate 64, behind lavatory 6, on Ajyad Street next to lavatory 1, and in the services building of the Third Saudi Expansion. Collection points are also reported at Gate 1, Gate 79, Bab al-Umrah and Gate 100.
  • Keep the ticket they give you — you need it to collect the bag. You may also be asked for your ID or Umrah permit on the app.
  • Paid left-luggage shops and lockers (roughly SAR 10–20 per bag per day) can be found around Ajyad, on Ibrahim Khalil Road near Gate 79 and in hotel lobbies, if you need longer than the free time.
  • Large bags are not allowed inside the Mataf or the courtyards, and security can refuse them at the gates — so drop them before you enter.

Children — wristbands, strollers, if a child is lost

  • Safety wristbands: free bands with the child's details and a parent's phone number are given out round the clock at the main gate areas (reported at the Ajyad gate area and at King Fahd Gate 79). Staff and volunteers can scan the band to bring the child back to you.
  • Do this yourself as well: write your mobile number on the child's wrist in Arabic numerals, and put a card in their pocket with the hotel name (in Arabic and English) and your number.
  • Children Centre: the Haram authority runs a free, supervised children's hospitality centre for visitors — booking is done online through the al-Haramain e-services portal, and the unified number is 1966.
  • If a child goes missing: do not search alone — go to the nearest security officer or uniformed steward straight away. They radio the lost-persons office, so the whole team starts looking for the child.
  • Agree a meeting point before you go in: a numbered gate plus a numbered lamp post, and photograph it — that is the easiest spot to find each other in the crowd.
  • Baby strollers are not allowed on the ground-floor Mataf because of the crowd; they are fine on the upper floors, which are much easier with children. A baby carrier is the most practical option for Tawaf and Sa'i. Nursing rooms are available.

Elderly parents — wheelchairs & electric carts

  • Free manual wheelchairs are provided for pilgrims who need them, and 13 gates are specially fitted with ramps for wheelchairs. The King Fahd expansion also has dedicated prayer halls for people with disabilities.
  • Paid carts for Tawaf and Sa'i are available if walking is difficult. Indicative prices: about SAR 50 for Tawaf, SAR 50 for Sa'i and around SAR 300 for a complete Umrah — usually free for verified disability and for children under six, with a discount for those over 65. Prices change, so confirm at the counter.
  • Where to get a cart: reported points include the eastern courtyard near Gate 19, the western courtyard at Shubaika Bridge (Gate 64), the ground floor of the Mas'a near Gate 14, the first floor of the Mas'a at the Arqam staircase (Gate 16), the southern courtyard near lavatory 2 and Ajyad Bridge near Gate 4. The counters are visible in the courtyards.
  • If your parents use a wheelchair, keep them on the upper floors if possible — the crowds are lighter, the lifts and escalators make it easier and there is more space to move.

Other facilities you can use

  • Shoes: numbered racks are inside most gates, but they get crowded. Carry a cloth bag so you can leave your shoes with you and exit by any gate; cheap flip-flops are sold just outside.
  • Toilets and wudu: there are no public toilets inside the mosque — the toilet and ablution complexes are underground below the courtyards, numbered and clearly signed. Wudu stations are also inside near the main Mataf entrances.
  • Women's prayer areas are on every level, marked with low partitions — the upper floors have the largest and calmest areas.
  • Gates close before the prayer — roughly one hour before, and they reopen about 30 minutes after. On Fridays and in Ramadan, arrive an hour or more early. The upper floors are the easiest place to be with children.
Practical Tips

Making Your Umrah Easier

Small preparations that save a lot of difficulty on the day.

Carry with you

  • A small pouch or belt for your Ihram and valuables (money, ID, phone).
  • Unscented tissues, a light water bottle, and comfortable flip-flops or sandals that leave your ankles uncovered.
  • A cloth shoe bag, and a card with your hotel name and your phone number.

Timing & comfort

  • As practical advice (not a religious ruling), the courtyards are usually lighter late at night after Isha and in the mid-morning, and heaviest after Fajr, after Maghrib and on Fridays.
  • Tawaf and Sa'i together normally take 1.5–3 hours; the Sa'i corridor alone is about 3 km. Plan the bus timings with that in mind.
  • Eat something light before you start, drink plenty of Zamzam, and rest between Tawaf and Sa'i if you are tired.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Cutting the hair before completing Sa'i — then you are still in Ihram.
  • Doing Tawaf without wudu, or starting a circuit away from the Black Stone — that circuit does not count.
  • Passing between the Kaaba and the Hateem wall during Tawaf.
  • Trying to kiss the Kaaba itself, or kissing the Yemeni corner — only the Black Stone is kissed, and the Yemeni corner is touched only.
  • Pushing and crowding at the Black Stone. If it is crowded, pointing from a distance is exactly what the Sunnah allows.
  • Following a printed "dua for each circuit" — there is no fixed dua per circuit. Say what you know from the heart.
  • Announcing the intention out loud at the Black Stone — the niyyah is in the heart.
Pilgrim Questions

Umrah Questions We Are Asked Most

? Do I need an Umrah permit before travelling?
Permits for Umrah are booked through the official Nusuk app when they are required — for example during the Hajj season. The rule can change, so check the current requirement before your travel date, or ask us and we will guide you.
? Can I do Tawaf on the upper floors instead of the Mataf?
Yes. Tawaf is valid anywhere inside Masjid al-Haram — ground floor or upper floors. It is not valid outside the mosque. The upper floors are a good choice for families, elderly parents and anyone who finds the Mataf crowd difficult.
? Can I pray inside the Hateem (Hijr Ismail)?
Yes — praying inside the Hateem is from the Sunnah, and no special permission is needed. Just remember that during Tawaf you must walk around the outside of the wall, never between the wall and the Kaaba.
? Can we take a baby stroller or a wheelchair into the Haram?
Wheelchairs are welcome — free manual wheelchairs and paid electric carts are available at the courtyards, and 13 gates have ramps. Baby strollers are not allowed on the ground-floor Mataf because of the crowd, but they are allowed on the upper floors.
? Where can we keep our luggage while we perform Umrah?
If you travel with us, your luggage stays in the bus or in your hotel. Inside the Haram there is also free official storage (about 4 hours, bags only, around 7 kg) plus paid left-luggage shops nearby. Large bags cannot be taken into the Mataf or the courtyards.
? What should we do if a child gets lost in the crowd?
Go to the nearest security officer or steward immediately — they radio the lost-persons office. Make it much easier in advance: take a free safety wristband from the gate area, or write your phone number on the child's wrist and keep a hotel card in their pocket.
A note on this guide: the rituals above follow the well-known Sunnah. Gate numbers, storage points, cart prices and crowd routes are managed by the Haram authority and do change from time to time — please confirm on the day with the staff or signage, and feel free to message our team on WhatsApp for help. If you have a detailed fiqh question, ask a qualified scholar you trust.
